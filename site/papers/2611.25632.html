What One Sample Cannot See: Why Fewer Denoising Steps Can Score Higher on Physics Benchmarks

首次收录：2026-11-20 · arXiv 提交：2026-11-19 UTC · 代码状态：未核实

摘要（arXiv 元数据）

When a storm can head north or south, a forecast down the middle may score better under squared error than either plausible path. The same paradox appears when video generators are tested as world models: fewer denoising steps can raise physics scores even as videos blur or stop moving early. Why? Physics-IQ compares each generated video with one recording, although a scene can have multiple valid futures. An exact decomposition shows its scores can reward reduced variation among generated futures, even when their distribution becomes less accurate. We introduce Kernel Physics-IQ to remove this incentive in expectation, needing only two independent samples per condition. Two-step sampling lowers Kernel Physics-IQ in all five image-to-video models. For CogVideoX, the official score rises by 0.055 while ours falls by 0.131. Changing the sampling schedule reveals further differences official scores miss. In controlled physical systems with known outcome probabilities, our metric favors the true distribution over collapsed predictions.
