What Do Learned 3D Models Add to Localization from Posed References?

首次收录：2026-11-20 · arXiv 提交：2026-11-18 UTC · 代码状态：未核实

几何基础模型

摘要（arXiv 元数据）

Localizing a query from a few posed reference images avoids building a 3D point map. We ask what the camera predictions of learned 3D models add to geometric estimation from the same references, and where failures arise. Across eight learned models and variants and five test sets, we compare reference budgets, swap pose components, and intervene on the references a model sees. At fine pose-error thresholds, geometric estimation with one additional reference is significantly more accurate than every model on two references on Cambridge and NAVER B1 and never significantly worse, alone or as a fallback behind a shared acceptance gate; with the same three references, the more accurate approach depends on the scene. The gain from the learned translation magnitude depends on reference selection: it improves seven of the eight models on 12-Scenes with the spread-out pairs we select, but none of the five we test with the two top-ranked retrieved references. Where retrieval also returns references from other locations, at NAVER Gangnam Station, models fail at different stages.
