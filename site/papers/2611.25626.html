Physical Laws without Physics Loss? Probing Learned Dynamics in Video World Models through Forced Response

首次收录：2026-11-22 · arXiv 提交：2026-11-19 UTC · 代码状态：未核实

摘要（arXiv 元数据）

Do video world models learn physical laws without a physics loss, or merely imitate motion? Overall motion and appearance scores cannot settle it. We investigate this through nonlinear forced response: an excitation shakes the support of a suspended object. We introduce dual visual-numeric conditioning and adapt six image-to-video backbones to predict this motion using only the flow-matching loss. Wan predicts motion accurately and generalizes to unseen excitations, objects, poses, and scenes. Counterfactual tests suggest capabilities beyond replaying training videos. But what laws have they learned? We design three tiers of tests using Taylor expansions of the dynamics and geometry. All six reproduce the linear response, including resonance; five capture higher-order dynamics, including nonlinear softening. Remarkably, softening is barely present in training: only 0.74% of the swing's squared displacement. Extensions to a double pendulum and a rocking block show sustained dynamics are learned more faithfully than brief impacts.
